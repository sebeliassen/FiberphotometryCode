## 3. Major Scientific Takeaways & Structural Discoveries

### A. The Grooming Module & Syntactic Gating (Syllables 22 $\longrightarrow$ 27 / 44 / 52 / 58–66)
- **Latent Space Clustering & Expanded Repertoire**: Syllables **44, 52, 58, 60, 61, 63, 65, and 66** alongside **27** form an interconnected grooming sub-network accounting for **$5.13\%$ of all cohort behavior**.
- **Empirical Transition Syntax**:
  - **Syllable 52 $\to$ 27 ($65.0\%$)**: Syllable 52 serves as a direct upstream feeder into primary body grooming (27).
  - **Syllable 61 $\to$ 66 ($21.7\%$) and 44 ($17.0\%$)**: Connects general body grooming with cephalic/forepaw grooming (66, "some hands").
  - **Syllable 44 $\to$ 27 ($17.6\%$)**: Re-circulates back into main torso maintenance.
- **Syntactic Gating via Syllables 22 and 28**:
  - Syllable 22 (`look right towards hind`) exhibits a $14.2\%$ transition probability into the grooming module, serving as a postural gateway.
  - Syllable 28 (`look left`) exhibits a $27.3\%$ transition probability into grooming, confirming bilateral orienting entry points before torso licking.

### B. Grooming Syntactic Rigidity: The Berridge Literature Precedent
- Berridge and colleagues (*BMC Biology* 2005; 3:4) demonstrated that hyper-dopaminergic DAT-knockdown mice exhibit increased completion and stereotyped rigidity of sequential grooming chains, as well as heightened resistance to interruption and disruption.
- This provides an essential literature-grounded framework: rather than merely measuring total grooming time, our 9-syllable grooming taxonomy allows us to test whether dopamine mutant (KI) animals exhibit altered syntax completion, chain progression, or interruption rates relative to Wild-Type controls.

### C. The Flagship Presentation Model: The Rear–Descent Locomotor Family
- Nominal Motif 1 was labeled as $14 \to 4 \to 3$, but retrieved episode analysis demonstrates that it is actually a **behavioral phrase family** anchored on a shared postural transition:
  - $14 \to 4 \to 3$ (`walk left`, $95$ episodes across 10 animals)
  - $14 \to 4 \to 17$ (`run left`, $79$ episodes across 10 animals)
  - $14 \to 4 \to 6$ (`turn left`, $37$ episodes across 9 animals)
- **Biological Principle**: *A shared postural transition can lead into several related executions.*
- **Fiber Photometry Application**: This provides a clean within-syllable decision point: align dopamine transients to the touchdown moment (Syllable 4) and test whether phasic DA predicts whether the animal commits to a walk ($3$), sprint ($17$), or turn ($6$).

### D. The Subtle Scanning Oscillation: Syllables 34 $\longleftrightarrow$ 49
- Syllable 34 (`look left [from right], very subtle`) and Syllable 49 (`stationary`) form a tightly coupled oscillatory pair:
  $$\text{Syllable 34} \xrightarrow{\mathbf{34.4\%}} \text{Syllable 49} \quad \text{and} \quad \text{Syllable 49} \xrightarrow{\mathbf{42.5\%}} \text{Syllable 34}$$
- Rather than an artifact or locomotor movement, this represents an active visual sampling mechanism where the mouse maintains a stationary body base while executing periodic subtle glances.

### E. The Sharp-Turning Bilateral Mirror (Syllables 29 & 30)
- Syllable 29 is annotated as **`walk left (sharp left)`** and Syllable 30 as **`walk right (sharp right)`**.
- This resolves why Syllable 30 exhibited extreme velocity ($71.3\text{ cm/s}$): it is a high-speed centrifugal turn rather than a linear sprint, perfectly mirroring Syllable 29 on the left!

### F. Locomotor & Stationary Stratification

### G. Model Boundaries & Misc Blends (Syllables 34 & 40)
- The observation that around Syllables 34 and 40 "stuff is getting very misc'y down here" correctly reflects the Dirichlet Process prior in Keypoint-MoSeq.
- The highest-probability states (0–29) capture clean, stereotyped kinematic primitives. As bout frequencies drop into Tier 2 ($<1\%$), the states increasingly capture transitional blends and boundary mixtures before dropping into the rare tail.

### H. The Clustering Granularity Trade-Off: "The Goldilocks Dilemma"
- **Why Clustering Helps**: Clustering syllables definitely improves sequence discovery by mitigating the model's hyper-fragmentation of redundant sub-states. For example, grouping the 9 grooming syllables ($27, 44, 52, 58\text{--}66$) into a unified `Grooming` module, or merging quiet resting states ($32, 43, 49$), drastically boosts seqNMF signal-to-noise ratio, eliminates trivial micro-alternations, and reveals large-scale behavioral grammar spanning 4 to 8 seconds.
- **Where Over-Clustering Fails (The 180° Turn vs. Slight Veer Trap)**: However, clustering can also be taken too far. Over-clustering collapses vital ethological and kinematic distinctions. For instance:
  - A **180° hairpin abort turn** executed at an arena wall represents a high-curvature obstacle evasion, threat avoidance, or boundary collision response.
  - A **slight 15° lateral veer** during open-field cruising represents minor course maintenance while preserving forward momentum.
  - If an unsupervised clustering algorithm collapses both into a monolithic "Turn" meta-syllable, crucial functional information is destroyed. The animal is expressing two entirely different cognitive and motor decisions.
- **The "Goldilocks" Rule (Selective Functional Clustering)**:
  1. *Cluster aggressively* for nearly identical, interchangeable postural maintenance states (such as grooming substeps, resting wakefulness, or bilateral sniffing twitches).
  2. *Cluster conservatively* for dynamic, goal-directed actions where angular amplitude, curvature sign, and velocity gradients encode distinct behavioral intent (distinguishing sharp abort turns from smooth veers, and explosive sprints from modest walking).
  3. This selective clustering strategy provides the most powerful, interpretable grammar for describing mouse behavior without sacrificing motor resolution.

### I. Cross-Genotype Behavioral "Replicas": An Invariant Tool for Fiber Photometry
- **Empirical Observation**: When inspecting our 1×3 widescreen Whole-Chain Exemplars across the cohort, a striking ethological phenomenon emerges: **Wild-Type (WT) and dopamine mutant (KI) mice frequently execute near-verbatim replicas of complex, multi-syllable behavioral programs**.
  - In **Macro-Motif 7 (Kinetic Wall-Rear & Sprint Loop)**, Panel 1 (KI `1658`), Panel 2 (WT `728`), and Panel 3 (KI `1666`) trace virtually identical kinematic arcs: accelerating along the wall, rearing vertically, dropping back down, and sprinting into open space.
  - In **Macro-Motif 4 (High-Speed Slalom Burst)**, Panel 1 (KI `1666`), Panel 2 (WT `1680`), and Panel 3 (WT `1765`) execute identical rapid lateral cuts and acceleration bursts.
- **The Motor Confound Problem in Photometry**: In behavioral neuroscience, interpreting dopamine (DA) or calcium photometric signals between genotypes is notoriously plagued by motor confounds. If a hyper-dopaminergic mutant exhibits altered DA transient peaks, is that change due to abnormal reward/prediction error signaling, or simply because the mutant mouse ran faster, turned harder, or moved more often?
- **The Analytical Breakthrough (Motor-Invariant Photometric Comparison)**:
  - By identifying **Cross-Genotype Behavioral Replicas**—exact instances where WT and KI mice execute the identical multi-syllable motor program with matched velocity and curvature profiles—we hold physical behavior completely constant.
  - Aligning fiber photometry signals (e.g. nucleus accumbens or dorsal striatum DA) to these matched behavioral replicas allows researchers to compare neural dynamics during identical motor actions.
  - Any observed divergence in DA peak amplitude, rise kinetics, or pre-action ramping can then be attributed purely to genotype-specific neuromodulatory state, effort valuation, or state-transition encoding, completely immune to movement confounds!

### J. Egocentric Posture vs. Allocentric Arena Grounding: The "Contortion Impostor" Solution
- **The Contortion Impostor Problem**: Because Keypoint-MoSeq models posture and body curvature egocentrically (invariant to translation and orientation), a mouse sitting stationary in a corner or wiggling in place can contort its spine in a sequence of curvatures that mathematically mimics a sprint-turn sequence, despite exhibiting near-zero displacement across the arena floor.
- **The Multimodal Kinematic Gate**:
  - To eliminate these impostors, we implemented a **20th-percentile Kinematic Consistency Gate** ($P_{20}$ path length, velocity, and minimum net displacement for locomotor motifs; $P_{80}$ ceiling for stationary resting motifs).
  - Furthermore, candidate episodes are projected onto a **220-dimensional Joint Multimodal Manifold** $[\mathbf{Z}_{\text{slds}}, \mathbf{V}]$ combining 200D continuous SLDS latent postures with 20D allocentric ground speed profiles.
  - Exemplar selection via Whole-Chain Contrastive Density Ratio ensures that chosen episodes are both posturally prototypical and spatially authentic.
